### Section 1: States of Matter & Phase Changes
*Reference: Slides 4-7*

**Problem 1: Entry Level (State Identification)**
A substance has a fixed volume but takes the shape of its container. Its particles slide past one another. Which state of matter is this?
- **Identify:** Fixed volume, variable shape.
- **Classify:** This matches the properties of a liquid.
- **Explain:** Solids have fixed shapes; gases have no fixed volume.
- **Result:** Liquid.

**Problem 2: Medium Level (Phase Change Energy)**
Water boils at $100^\circ\text{C}$, turning from a liquid into a gas. Is energy being absorbed or released by the water during this process?
- **Identify:** Liquid $\rightarrow$ Gas (Vaporization).
- **Classify:** Vaporization is an endothermic process.
- **Explain:** To break the attractions between liquid particles so they can move freely as a gas, energy must be added to the system.
- **Result:** Energy is absorbed.

**Problem 3: Difficult Level (Clinical Application - Evaporative Cooling)**
During a high fever, a patient's skin is damp with sweat. As the sweat evaporates, the patient feels a cooling sensation. Explain the physics of this process.
- **Identify:** Liquid sweat $\rightarrow$ Water vapor (Gas).
- **Analysis:** Evaporation requires energy (heat).
- **Explain:** The water molecules absorb the necessary heat energy directly from the patient's skin to make the phase change. As heat leaves the skin to power the evaporation, the skin's temperature drops.
- **Result:** Evaporative cooling occurs because vaporization absorbs heat from the skin.

### Section 2: Classifying Matter
*Reference: Slides 8-11*

**Problem 4: Entry Level (Pure Substance vs. Mixture)**
Is a piece of pure 24K gold (Element: Au) a pure substance or a mixture?
- **Identify:** Gold is a single type of atom.
- **Classify:** Pure substance (specifically an element).
- **Explain:** It cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical means.
- **Result:** Pure substance.

**Problem 5: Medium Level (Mixture Classification)**
Normal saline IV fluid is a uniform mixture of salt and water. Is this a homogeneous or heterogeneous mixture?
- **Identify:** Uniform composition throughout.
- **Classify:** Homogeneous mixture (also known as a solution).
- **Explain:** You cannot see the individual salt particles; the mixture looks the same from top to bottom.
- **Result:** Homogeneous mixture.

**Problem 6: Difficult Level (Separation Methods)**
A medical lab needs to separate a drug from a complex biological sample based on the different speeds the drug travels through a specialized medium. Which separation technique are they using?
- **Identify:** Separation based on speed through a medium.
- **Classify:** Chromatography.
- **Explain:** Chromatography leverages differences in chemical reactivity or particle size to separate components as they move through a stationary phase.
- **Result:** Chromatography.

### Section 3: Physical vs. Chemical Changes
*Reference: Slides 12-14*

**Problem 7: Entry Level (Physical Change)**
A nurse crushes a tablet into a powder to administer it via a feeding tube. Is this a physical or chemical change?
- **Identify:** Change in size and shape; composition remains the same.
- **Classify:** Physical change.
- **Explain:** The medicine is still the same chemical; only its form has changed.
- **Result:** Physical change.

**Problem 8: Medium Level (Chemical Change Indicators)**
A clear solution is added to another clear solution, and suddenly a cloudy white solid forms. What has occurred, and what is the chemistry term for that solid?
- **Identify:** Formation of a new solid from two liquids.
- **Classify:** Chemical change.
- **Explain:** The formation of a "precipitate" is a classic sign that a chemical reaction has occurred, creating a new substance that is insoluble in the liquid.
- **Result:** Chemical change; the solid is a precipitate.

**Problem 9: Difficult Level (Mixed Analysis)**
Iron rusting on a medical cart is a chemical change, while ice melting in a specimen cup is a physical change. Contrast these two based on the "reversibility" and "composition" rules.
- **Analysis (Rust):** Iron reacts with oxygen $\rightarrow$ new substance (iron oxide) $\rightarrow$ difficult to reverse.
- **Analysis (Ice):** $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (solid) $\rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O}$ (liquid) $\rightarrow$ composition is unchanged $\rightarrow$ easy to reverse by freezing.
- **Result:** Chemical changes create new substances and are hard to reverse; physical changes only alter form and are typically easy to reverse.

### Section 4: Conservation of Mass and Energy
*Reference: Slides 15-16*

**Problem 10: Entry Level (Conservation of Mass)**
If $10\text{ g}$ of a reactant A reacts completely with $5\text{ g}$ of reactant B in a sealed flask, what will be the total mass of the products?
- **Identify:** Total starting mass $= 10\text{ g} + 5\text{ g} = 15\text{ g}$.
- **Law:** Law of Conservation of Mass.
- **Explain:** Mass is neither created nor destroyed.
- **Result:** $15\text{ g}$.

**Problem 11: Medium Level (Conservation of Energy)**
The body converts chemical energy from glucose into kinetic energy (movement) and thermal energy (heat). Does the total amount of energy change during this process?
- **Identify:** Energy transformation (Chemical $\rightarrow$ Kinetic + Thermal).
- **Law:** Law of Conservation of Energy.
- **Explain:** Energy only changes form; it is not created or destroyed.
- **Result:** No, the total energy remains the same.

**Problem 12: Difficult Level (Application of Mass Conservation)**
In a sealed container, $10\text{ g}$ of wood burns in oxygen. After the fire, only $2\text{ g}$ of ash remains. Does this violate the Law of Conservation of Mass? Explain.
- **Identify:** Starting mass (wood + oxygen) $\rightarrow$ Ending mass (ash + gases).
- **Analysis:** $10\text{ g}$ wood $\neq 2\text{ g}$ ash.
- **Explain:** The "missing" mass escaped as carbon dioxide and water vapor gases. Because the container is sealed, these gases are still inside.
- **Result:** No, it does not violate the law. The total mass of ash plus the produced gases equals the original mass of wood plus oxygen.
