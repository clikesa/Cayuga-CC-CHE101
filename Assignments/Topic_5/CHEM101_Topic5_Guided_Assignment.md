### Section 1: Organizing the Periodic Table
*Reference: Slides 4-8*

**Problem 1: Entry Level (Basic Structure)**
What is the difference between a "period" and a "group" on the periodic table?
- **Identify:** Period = row; Group = column.
- **Explain:** A period is a horizontal row (numbered 1-7); as you move across, elements gain one proton and electron. A group is a vertical column (numbered 1-18); elements in the same group share the same number of valence electrons and similar chemical behavior.
- **Result:** Period = horizontal row; Group = vertical column.

**Problem 2: Medium Level (Group Identification)**
An element is found in Group 17. What is the common name for this group, and what is a characteristic property of these elements?
- **Identify:** Group 17 = Halogens.
- **Classify:** Highly reactive nonmetals.
- **Explain:** Halogens have 7 valence electrons and are very reactive because they only need one more electron to complete their outer shell.
- **Result:** Halogens; highly reactive nonmetals.

**Problem 3: Difficult Level (Historical Context)**
How did Moseley's correction to Mendeleev's table change the way the periodic table is organized, and why is this significant?
- **Identify:** Mendeleev organized by atomic mass; Moseley organized by atomic number.
- **Explain:** By ordering elements by atomic number (number of protons), Moseley fixed a few mismatches where mass didn't align with properties. This established the Modern Periodic Law.
- **Result:** Shifted organization from atomic mass to atomic number.

### Section 2: Periodic Trends
*Reference: Slides 9-12*

**Problem 4: Entry Level (Atomic Radius Trend)**
As you move from left to right across a period (e.g., from Lithium to Neon), does the atomic radius generally increase or decrease? Why?
- **Identify:** Left $\rightarrow$ Right trend.
- **Classify:** Atomic radius decreases.
- **Explain:** As you move right, more protons are added to the nucleus, increasing the positive pull on the electrons in the same energy level, drawing them in tighter.
- **Result:** Decreases due to increased nuclear pull.

**Problem 5: Medium Level (Ionization Energy)**
Why does ionization energy increase as you move from left to right across a period?
- **Identify:** Left $\rightarrow$ Right trend.
- **Classify:** Ionization energy increases.
- **Explain:** Because the atomic radius is smaller and the nuclear pull is stronger (more protons), it requires more energy to remove the outermost electron from the atom.
- **Result:** Increases because electrons are held more tightly by the nucleus.

**Problem 6: Difficult Level (Ions and Radius)**
Compare a neutral Sodium (Na) atom to a Sodium ion ($\text{Na}^+$). Which one is larger, and why?
- **Identify:** Neutral atom vs. Cation.
- **Classify:** The neutral atom is larger.
- **Explain:** Sodium loses its outermost electron to become $\text{Na}^+$. In doing so, it loses its entire outer energy shell, which significantly reduces its radius.
- **Result:** Neutral Na is larger; $\text{Na}^+$ has lost an entire shell.

### Section 3: Ions and Charges
*Reference: Slides 13-14*

**Problem 7: Entry Level (Predicting Cations)**
An element is in Group 2A. What is its most likely ionic charge when it forms an ion?
- **Identify:** Group 2A (Alkaline Earth Metals).
- **Classify:** Positive charge.
- **Explain:** Group 2 elements have 2 valence electrons; they prefer to lose those 2 electrons to achieve a stable full shell.
- **Result:** $+2$.

**Problem 8: Medium Level (Predicting Anions)**
An element is in Group 6A. What is its most likely ionic charge?
- **Identify:** Group 6A (Nonmetals).
- **Classify:** Negative charge.
- **Explain:** Group 6 elements have 6 valence electrons. They need 2 more to reach a full octet (8), so they gain 2 electrons.
- **Result:** $-2$.

**Problem 9: Difficult Level (Clinical Link - Electrolytes)**
Potassium ($\text{K}^+$) and Calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$) are critical electrolytes in the body. Using their positions on the periodic table, explain why they form these specific charges.
- **Analysis (K):** Potassium is in Group 1; it has 1 valence electron $\rightarrow$ loses 1 $\rightarrow$ charge $+1$.
- **Analysis (Ca):** Calcium is in Group 2; it has 2 valence electrons $\rightarrow$ loses 2 $\rightarrow$ charge $+2$.
- **Result:** Charges are determined by the group number (number of valence electrons lost).

### Section 4: Electron Dot (Lewis) Notation
*Reference: Slides 15-16*

**Problem 10: Entry Level (Valence Count)**
How many valence electrons does an atom of Aluminum (Al), which is in Group 13, have?
- **Identify:** Group 13.
- **Rule:** For main group A elements, valence electrons = group number (13 $\rightarrow$ 3).
- **Result:** 3 valence electrons.

**Problem 11: Medium Level (Drawing Dots)**
Describe how you would draw the electron dot diagram for Carbon (C), which is in Group 14.
- **Identify:** 4 valence electrons.
- **Process:** Place one dot on each of the four sides of the "C" symbol (top, bottom, left, right).
- **Result:** Carbon symbol with 4 single dots (one on each side).

**Problem 12: Difficult Level (Reactivity & Dots)**
Chlorine (Cl) is in Group 17. Draw its dot diagram and explain why this specific arrangement makes chlorine highly reactive.
- **Identify:** 7 valence electrons.
- **Process:** Place one dot on each of the 4 sides, then add 3 more to pair them up. This leaves one side with a single, unpaired electron.
- **Explain:** The presence of one "hole" (unpaired electron) makes the atom unstable. It will react aggressively to gain one more electron to complete the octet.
- **Result:** Cl with 7 dots; highly reactive due to the one unpaired electron.
