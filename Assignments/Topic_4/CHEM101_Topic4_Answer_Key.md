### Section 1: History of Atomic Models

**Problem 1:**
- Thomson: Positive sphere with electrons embedded.
- Rutherford: Mostly empty space with a tiny, dense, positive nucleus.
- Discovery: The gold foil experiment (some particles bounced back).

**Problem 2:**
- Bohr proposed fixed circular orbits. The Quantum model recognizes that we can only know the probability of finding an electron in a region (orbital), not its exact path.

**Problem 3:**
- John Dalton. He based it on Lavoisier's precise measurements of mass in reactions.

### Section 2: Atomic Notation and Mass

**Problem 4:**
- Element: Chlorine (Cl).
- Protons: 17.
- Electrons: 17.
- Neutrons: $35 - 17 = 18$.

**Problem 5:**
- They have the same number of protons (17) and electrons (17), but different numbers of neutrons (35 vs 37).

**Problem 6:**
- Mass Number $= 26\text{ protons} + 30\text{ neutrons} = 56$.

### Section 3: Energy Levels & Orbitals

**Problem 7:**
- Total: 8 electrons.
- Breakdown: $2s$ sublevel (2) and $2p$ sublevel (6).

**Problem 8:**
- Energy level: 3.
- Sublevel: d.

**Problem 9:**
- Because the 2s sublevel has lower energy than the 2p sublevel. The Aufbau Principle states electrons fill the lowest energy levels first.

### Section 4: Electron Configurations

**Problem 10:**
- **$1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2$**

**Problem 11:**
- **$1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^4$**

**Problem 12:**
- Neutral Ca: $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 4s^2$.
- $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ loses 2 electrons (from the highest level).
