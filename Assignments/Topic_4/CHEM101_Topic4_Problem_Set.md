# CHEM 101 Topic 4: Atomic Structure — Problem Set

**Instructions:** Complete the following problems using the analysis method practiced in your guided assignment. Show all your work, including your reasoning for the atomic models and your step-by-step filling of electron configurations.

---

### Section 1: History of Atomic Models

**Problem 1:** Contrast the "Plum Pudding" model of J.J. Thomson with the model proposed by Ernest Rutherford. What was the key discovery that led to the change?

**Problem 2:** Why is the Quantum Model more accurate than the Bohr model when describing the location of an electron?

**Problem 3:** Which early scientist proposed that the atom was a solid, indivisible sphere, and on what basis did he create this theory?

---

### Section 2: Atomic Notation and Mass

**Problem 4:** An atom has an atomic number of 17 and a mass number of 35. Identify the element and determine the number of protons, neutrons, and electrons in a neutral atom of this element.

**Problem 5:** Chlorine has two common isotopes: Chlorine-35 and Chlorine-37. How do these two isotopes differ in terms of their subatomic particles?

**Problem 6:** Calculate the mass number of an isotope that contains 26 protons and 30 neutrons.

---

### Section 3: Energy Levels & Orbitals

**Problem 7:** What is the maximum number of electrons that can occupy the second energy level (n=2) in total? Break this down by sublevel (s and p).

**Problem 8:** If an electron is in a "3d" orbital, what is its energy level and what is its sublevel?

**Problem 9:** Explain why an electron cannot enter the 2p sublevel until the 2s sublevel is completely filled. Which principle explains this?

---

### Section 4: Electron Configurations

**Problem 10:** Write the full electron configuration for Magnesium (Mg), which has an atomic number of 12.

**Problem 11:** Write the full electron configuration for Sulfur (S), which has an atomic number of 16.

**Problem 12:** Write the electron configuration for a Calcium ion ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$), given that neutral Calcium has an atomic number of 20.
