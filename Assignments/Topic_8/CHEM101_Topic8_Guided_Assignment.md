### Section 1: Avogadro's Number and the Mole
*Reference: Slides 5-6*

**Problem 1: Entry Level (Particles to Moles)**
A sample contains $1.204 \times 10^{24}$ atoms of Gold (Au). How many moles of gold are present?
- **Given:** $1.204 \times 10^{24}$ atoms.
- **Bridge:** Avogadro's number: $1 \text{ mol} = 6.022 \times 10^{23}$ particles.
- **Calculate:** $1.204 \times 10^{24} \text{ atoms} \div 6.022 \times 10^{23} \text{ atoms/mol} \approx 2.00 \text{ mol}$.
- **Check:** The number of atoms is roughly double Avogadro's number, so 2 moles makes sense.
- **Result:** $2.00 \text{ mol}$.

**Problem 2: Medium Level (Moles to Particles)**
How many molecules are in $0.250 \text{ moles}$ of Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$)?
- **Given:** $0.250 \text{ mol}$.
- **Bridge:** $1 \text{ mol} = 6.022 \times 10^{23}$ particles.
- **Calculate:** $0.250 \text{ mol} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} \text{ molecules/mol} = 1.51 \times 10^{23} \text{ molecules}$.
- **Check:** $0.25$ is a quarter of a mole; the result should be a quarter of Avogadro's number.
- **Result:** $1.51 \times 10^{23} \text{ molecules}$.

**Problem 3: Difficult Level (Atomic vs Molecular Count)**
How many **total atoms** are in $1.00 \text{ mole}$ of Glucose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$)?
- **Given:** $1.00 \text{ mol}$ of $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$.
- **Bridge 1:** $1 \text{ mol} = 6.022 \times 10^{23}$ molecules.
- **Bridge 2:** One molecule of glucose contains $6 + 12 + 6 = 24$ atoms.
- **Calculate:** $1.00 \text{ mol} \times (6.022 \times 10^{23} \text{ molecules/mol}) \times (24 \text{ atoms/molecule}) = 1.45 \times 10^{25} \text{ atoms}$.
- **Check:** The total atom count must be significantly larger than Avogadro's number because each molecule contains many atoms.
- **Result:** $1.45 \times 10^{25} \text{ atoms}$.

### Section 2: Molar Mass and Mass-Mole Conversions
*Reference: Slides 8-11*

**Problem 4: Entry Level (Calculating Molar Mass)**
Calculate the molar mass of Sodium Chloride ($\text{NaCl}$).
- **Given:** Formula $\text{NaCl}$.
- **Bridge:** Atomic masses from Periodic Table: $\text{Na} = 22.99 \text{ g/mol}$, $\text{Cl} = 35.45 \text{ g/mol}$.
- **Calculate:** $22.99 + 35.45 = 58.44 \text{ g/mol}$.
- **Check:** The sum of the two elements matches the expected molar mass.
- **Result:** $58.44 \text{ g/mol}$.

**Problem 5: Medium Level (Grams to Moles)**
A patient is given $117 \text{ g}$ of $\text{NaCl}$ in an IV bag. How many moles of $\text{NaCl}$ is this?
- **Given:** Mass = $117 \text{ g}$; Molar Mass = $58.44 \text{ g/mol}$.
- **Bridge:** $\text{moles} = \text{grams} \div \text{molar mass}$.
- **Calculate:** $117 \text{ g} \div 58.44 \text{ g/mol} \approx 2.00 \text{ mol}$.
- **Check:** $117$ is roughly double $58.44$, so 2 moles is correct.
- **Result:** $2.00 \text{ mol}$.

**Problem 6: Difficult Level (Moles to Grams)**
A lab requires $0.050 \text{ moles}$ of Calcium Carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$). How many grams should be weighed?
- **Given:** Moles = $0.050 \text{ mol}$.
- **Bridge 1 (Molar Mass):** $\text{Ca}(40.08) + \text{C}(12.01) + 3 \times \text{O}(16.00) = 100.09 \text{ g/mol}$.
- **Bridge 2:** $\text{grams} = \text{moles} \times \text{molar mass}$.
- **Calculate:** $0.050 \text{ mol} \times 100.09 \text{ g/mol} = 5.00 \text{ g}$.
- **Check:** $0.05$ is $1/20$th of a mole; $100/20 = 5$. Correct.
- **Result:** $5.00 \text{ g}$.

### Section 3: Molarity
*Reference: Slides 13-14*

**Problem 7: Entry Level (Basic Molarity)**
Calculate the molarity of a solution containing $2.00 \text{ moles}$ of solute in $4.00 \text{ L}$ of solution.
- **Given:** Moles = $2.00$; Volume = $4.00 \text{ L}$.
- **Bridge:** $M = \text{moles} \div \text{liters}$.
- **Calculate:** $2.00 \text{ mol} \div 4.00 \text{ L} = 0.500 \text{ M}$.
- **Check:** Half a mole per liter.
- **Result:** $0.500 \text{ M}$.

**Problem 8: Medium Level (Mass to Molarity)**
What is the molarity of a solution prepared by dissolving $5.84 \text{ g}$ of $\text{NaCl}$ (molar mass $58.44 \text{ g/mol}$) in enough water to make $500 \text{ mL}$ of solution?
- **Given:** Mass = $5.84 \text{ g}$; Molar Mass = $58.44 \text{ g/mol}$; Volume = $500 \text{ mL} = 0.500 \text{ L}$.
- **Bridge 1 (Moles):** $5.84 \text{ g} \div 58.44 \text{ g/mol} \approx 0.100 \text{ mol}$.
- **Bridge 2 (Molarity):** $M = \text{moles} \div \text{liters}$.
- **Calculate:** $0.100 \text{ mol} \div 0.500 \text{ L} = 0.200 \text{ M}$.
- **Check:** A small amount of salt in a small volume results in a low but significant molarity.
- **Result:** $0.200 \text{ M}$.

**Problem 9: Difficult Level (Molarity to Mass)**
An IV bag contains $1.00 \text{ L}$ of $0.150 \text{ M}$ Glucose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$, molar mass $180.16 \text{ g/mol}$). How many grams of glucose are in the bag?
- **Given:** Volume = $1.00 \text{ L}$; Molarity = $0.150 \text{ mol/L}$; Molar Mass = $180.16 \text{ g/mol}$.
- **Bridge 1 (Moles):** $\text{moles} = \text{Molarity} \times \text{Volume} = 0.150 \text{ mol/L} \times 1.00 \text{ L} = 0.150 \text{ mol}$.
- **Bridge 2 (Mass):** $\text{grams} = \text{moles} \times \text{molar mass}$.
- **Calculate:** $0.150 \text{ mol} \times 180.16 \text{ g/mol} = 27.02 \text{ g}$.
- **Check:** $15\%$ of $180$ is roughly $27$. Correct.
- **Result:** $27.02 \text{ g}$.

### Section 4: Empirical and Molecular Formulas
*Reference: Slides 15-16*

**Problem 10: Entry Level (Empirical to Molecular)**
A compound has an empirical formula of $\text{CH}_2$ and a molecular formula of $\text{C}_4\text{H}_8$. What is the multiplier?
- **Given:** Empirical $\text{CH}_2$; Molecular $\text{C}_4\text{H}_8$.
- **Calculate:** Compare subscripts: $\text{C}: 4/1 = 4$; $\text{H}: 8/2 = 4$.
- **Result:** Multiplier = 4.

**Problem 11: Medium Level (Finding Molecular Formula)**
A compound has an empirical formula of $\text{NO}_2$ (molar mass $46.01 \text{ g/mol}$) and a measured molar mass of $92.02 \text{ g/mol}$. What is the molecular formula?
- **Given:** Empirical Molar Mass = $46.01 \text{ g/mol}$; Molecular Molar Mass = $92.02 \text{ g/mol}$.
- **Bridge:** $\text{Multiplier} = \text{Molecular Mass} \div \text{Empirical Mass}$.
- **Calculate:** $92.02 \div 46.01 = 2$.
- **Apply:** Multiply $\text{NO}_2$ subscripts by 2 $\rightarrow$ $\text{N}_2\text{O}_4$.
- **Result:** $\text{N}_2\text{O}_4$.

**Problem 12: Difficult Level (Scaling Complex Formula)**
A compound has an empirical formula of $\text{C}_2\text{H}_3\text{O}$ (molar mass $57.05 \text{ g/mol}$) and a measured molar mass of $171.15 \text{ g/mol}$. Determine the molecular formula.
- **Given:** Empirical Molar Mass = $57.05 \text{ g/mol}$; Molecular Molar Mass = $171.15 \text{ g/mol}$.
- **Calculate:** $171.15 \div 57.05 = 3$.
- **Apply:** Multiply $\text{C}_2\text{H}_3\text{O}$ subscripts by 3 $\rightarrow$ $\text{C}_6\text{H}_9\text{O}_3$.
- **Result:** $\text{C}_6\text{H}_9\text{O}_3$.
