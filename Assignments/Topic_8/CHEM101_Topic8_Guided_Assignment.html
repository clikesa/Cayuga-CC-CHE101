CHEM 101 Topic 8: Chemical Quantities
Guided Assignment

Instructions: This assignment focuses on the fundamental tools used to count atoms and molecules: the mole, molar mass, molarity, and formula scaling. These calculations are the bedrock of chemistry and healthcare dosing. To get the most out of this exercise, please work through these solutions yourself on a separate sheet of paper. Practice using the analysis steps (Given → Bridge → Calculate → Check) for each problem to build your muscle memory before attempting the graded problem set for Topic 8.
Section 1: Avogadro's Number and the Mole
Problem 1: Entry Level (Particles to Moles) A sample contains 1.204 × 1024 atoms of Gold (Au). How many moles of gold are present?
Given: 1.204 × 1024 atoms. Bridge: Avogadro's number: 1 mol = 6.022 × 1023 particles. Calculate: 1.204 × 1024 atoms ÷ 6.022 × 1023 atoms/mol ≈ 2.00 mol. Check: The number of atoms is roughly double Avogadro's number, so 2 moles makes sense.
Result: 2.00 mol.
Problem 2: Medium Level (Moles to Particles) How many molecules are in 0.250 moles of Water (H2O)?
Given: 0.250 mol. Bridge: 1 mol = 6.022 × 1023 particles. Calculate: 0.250 mol × 6.022 × 1023 molecules/mol = 1.51 × 1023 molecules. Check: 0.25 is a quarter of a mole; the result should be a quarter of Avogadro's number.
Result: 1.51 × 1023 molecules.
Problem 3: Difficult Level (Atomic vs Molecular Count) How many total atoms are in 1.00 mole of Glucose (C6H12O6)?
Given: 1.00 mol of C6H12O6. Bridge 1: 1 mol = 6.022 × 1023 molecules. Bridge 2: One molecule of glucose contains 6 + 12 + 6 = 24 atoms. Calculate: 1.00 mol × (6.022 × 1023 molecules/mol) × (24 atoms/molecule) = 1.45 × 1025 atoms. Check: The total atom count must be significantly larger than Avogadro's number because each molecule contains many atoms.
Result: 1.45 × 1025 atoms.
Section 2: Molar Mass and Mass-Mole Conversions
Problem 4: Entry Level (Calculating Molar Mass) Calculate the molar mass of Sodium Chloride (NaCl).
Given: Formula NaCl. Bridge: Atomic masses from Periodic Table: Na = 22.99 g/mol, Cl = 35.45 g/mol. Calculate: 22.99 + 35.45 = 58.44 g/mol. Check: The sum of the two elements matches the expected molar mass.
Result: 58.44 g/mol.
Problem 5: Medium Level (Grams to Moles) A patient is given 117 g of NaCl in an IV bag. How many moles of NaCl is this?
Given: Mass = 117 g; Molar Mass = 58.44 g/mol. Bridge: moles = grams ÷ molar mass. Calculate: 117 g ÷ 58.44 g/mol ≈ 2.00 mol. Check: 117 is roughly double 58.44, so 2 moles is correct.
Result: 2.00 mol.
Problem 6: Difficult Level (Moles to Grams) A lab requires 0.050 moles of Calcium Carbonate (CaCO3). How many grams should be weighed?
Given: Moles = 0.050 mol. Bridge 1 (Molar Mass): Ca(40.08) + C(12.01) + 3 × O(16.00) = 100.09 g/mol. Bridge 2: grams = moles × molar mass. Calculate: 0.050 mol × 100.09 g/mol = 5.00 g. Check: 0.05 is 1/20th of a mole; 100/20 = 5. Correct.
Result: 5.00 g.
Section 3: Molarity
Problem 7: Entry Level (Basic Molarity) Calculate the molarity of a solution containing 2.00 moles of solute in 4.00 L of solution.
Given: Moles = 2.00; Volume = 4.00 L. Bridge: M = moles ÷ liters. Calculate: 2.00 mol ÷ 4.00 L = 0.500 M. Check: Half a mole per liter.
Result: 0.500 M.
Problem 8: Medium Level (Mass to Molarity) What is the molarity of a solution prepared by dissolving 5.84 g of NaCl (molar mass 58.44 g/mol) in enough water to make 500 mL of solution?
Given: Mass = 5.84 g; Molar Mass = 58.44 g/mol; Volume = 500 mL = 0.500 L. Bridge 1 (Moles): 5.84 g ÷ 58.44 g/mol ≈ 0.100 mol. Bridge 2 (Molarity): M = moles ÷ liters. Calculate: 0.100 mol ÷ 0.500 L = 0.200 M. Check: A small amount of salt in a small volume results in a low but significant molarity.
Result: 0.200 M.
Problem 9: Difficult Level (Molarity to Mass) An IV bag contains 1.00 L of 0.150 M Glucose (C6H12O6, molar mass 180.16 g/mol). How many grams of glucose are in the bag?
Given: Volume = 1.00 L; Molarity = 0.150 mol/L; Molar Mass = 180.16 g/mol. Bridge 1 (Moles): moles = Molarity × Volume = 0.150 mol/L × 1.00 L = 0.150 mol. Bridge 2 (Mass): grams = moles × molar mass. Calculate: 0.150 mol × 180.16 g/mol = 27.02 g. Check: 15% of 180 is roughly 27. Correct.
Result: 27.02 g.
Section 4: Empirical and Molecular Formulas
Problem 10: Entry Level (Empirical to Molecular) A compound has an empirical formula of CH2 and a molecular formula of C4H8. What is the multiplier?
Given: Empirical CH2; Molecular C4H8. Calculate: Compare subscripts: C: 4/1 = 4; H: 8/2 = 4.
Result: Multiplier = 4.
Problem 11: Medium Level (Finding Molecular Formula) A compound has an empirical formula of NO2 (molar mass 46.01 g/mol) and a measured molar mass of 92.02 g/mol. What is the molecular formula?
Given: Empirical Molar Mass = 46.01 g/mol; Molecular Molar Mass = 92.02 g/mol. Bridge: Multiplier = Molecular Mass ÷ Empirical Mass. Calculate: 92.02 ÷ 46.01 = 2. Apply: Multiply NO2 subscripts by 2 → N2O4.
Result: N2O4.
Problem 12: Difficult Level (Scaling Complex Formula) A compound has an empirical formula of C2H3O (molar mass 57.05 g/mol) and a measured molar mass of 171.15 g/mol. Determine the molecular formula.
Given: Empirical Molar Mass = 57.05 g/mol; Molecular Molar Mass = 171.15 g/mol. Calculate: 171.15 ÷ 57.05 = 3. Apply: Multiply C2H3O subscripts by 3 → C6H9O3.
Result: C6H9O3.
