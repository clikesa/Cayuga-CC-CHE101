Section 1: The Metric System & Dimensional Analysis
Problem 1: Entry Level (Simple Conversion) A patient is prescribed 0.500 grams of a medication. How many milligrams (mg) is this?
Given: 0.500 g Desired: mg Factor: 1 g = 1,000 mg Set Up: 0.500 g × (1,000 mg / 1 g) Calculation: 0.500 × 1,000 = 500 mg Check: Converting from a larger unit (g) to a smaller unit (mg) should result in a larger number. 500 > 0.500. Correct.
Result: 500 mg
Problem 2: Medium Level (Clinical Application) A medication dose is listed as 125 mcg (micrograms). How many milligrams (mg) is this?
Given: 125 mcg Desired: mg Factor: 1,000 mcg = 1 mg Set Up: 125 mcg × (1 mg / 1,000 mcg) Calculation: 125 ÷ 1,000 = 0.125 mg Check: mcg is significantly smaller than mg, so the numerical value should decrease. Correct.
Result: 0.125 mg
Problem 3: Difficult Level (Multi-step Conversion) A laboratory sample requires 0.045 kg of a reagent. If the reagent is only available in milligram (mg) increments, how many milligrams must be weighed out?
Given: 0.045 kg Desired: mg Factors: 1 kg = 1,000 g and 1 g = 1,000 mg Set Up: 0.045 kg × (1,000 g / 1 kg) × (1,000 mg / 1 g) Calculation: 0.045 × 1,000 × 1,000 = 45,000 mg Check: kg is very large compared to mg, so the number should increase significantly. Correct.
Result: 45,000 mg
Section 2: Scientific Notation
Problem 4: Entry Level (Standard to Scientific) The speed of light is approximately 300,000,000 m/s. Write this in scientific notation.
Process: Move the decimal point to the left until you have a value between 1 and 10. Step: Move decimal 8 places left: 3.00 × 108 m/s.
Result: 3.00 × 108 m/s
Problem 5: Medium Level (Scientific to Standard) A sample has a mass of 4.15 × 10-4 g. Write this in standard decimal form.
Process: Negative exponent means move the decimal to the left. Step: Move decimal 4 places left: 0.000415 g.
Result: 0.000415 g
Problem 6: Difficult Level (Small Scale Precision) The diameter of a specific protein is 0.0000000052 meters. Write this in scientific notation and express it in nanometers (nm), where 1 nm = 10-9 m.
Step A (Scientific): Move decimal 9 places right: 5.2 × 10-9 m. Step B (Conversion): 5.2 × 10-9 m × (1 nm / 10-9 m) = 5.2 nm.
Result: 5.2 × 10-9 m or 5.2 nm
Section 3: Significant Figures
Problem 7: Entry Level (Counting Sig Figs) Determine the number of sig figs in the following measurements: a) 0.0045 g, b) 105.0 mL.
Analysis a: 0.0045 → 2 sig figs (leading zeros don't count). Analysis b: 105.0 → 4 sig figs (zeros between non-zeros and trailing zeros with a decimal count).
Problem 8: Medium Level (Basic Calculation Rounding) Calculate the area of a lab slide: 2.5 cm × 2.55 cm.
Calculation: 2.5 × 2.55 = 6.375 cm2. Rounding Rule: Round to the fewest total sig figs. Analysis: 2.5 has 2 sig figs; 2.55 has 3.
Result: 6.4 cm2
Problem 9: Difficult Level (Mixed Operation Rounding) A technician combines three solutions: 12.11 g, 1.1 g, and 0.045 g. They then divide the total mass by a volume of 5.0 mL. What is the final result rounded to the correct sig figs?
Step A (Addition): 12.11 + 1.1 + 0.045 = 13.255 g. Rounding A: Round to fewest decimal places (1 decimal place from 1.1). Result = 13.3 g. Step B (Division): 13.3 g ÷ 5.0 mL = 2.66 g/mL. Rounding B: Round to fewest total sig figs (2 sig figs from 5.0).
Result: 2.7 g/mL
Section 4: Temperature & Density
Problem 10: Entry Level (Temp Conversion) A solution is stored at 25°C. What is this temperature in Kelvin?
Formula: K = °C + 273 Calculation: 25 + 273 = 298 K
Result: 298 K
Problem 11: Medium Level (Density Calculation) A piece of metal has a mass of 27.0 g and a volume of 10.0 mL. Calculate the density.
Formula: Density = mass ÷ volume Calculation: 27.0 g ÷ 10.0 mL = 2.70 g/mL Sig Fig Check: Both inputs have 3 sig figs, so the result should have 3.
Result: 2.70 g/mL
Problem 12: Difficult Level (Clinical Interpretation) A 10.0 mL urine sample has a mass of 10.25 g. Calculate the density and determine if it falls within the normal clinical range of 1.005–1.030 g/mL. If the density were 1.050 g/mL, what might that suggest about the patient's hydration?
Calculation: 10.25 g ÷ 10.0 mL = 1.025 g/mL Interpretation: 1.025 is within the normal range. Advanced Analysis: A value of 1.050 g/mL is higher than normal, meaning the urine is more dense (more solutes/less water), which typically suggests dehydration.
Result: 1.025 g/mL (Normal)
