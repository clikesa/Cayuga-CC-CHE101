CHEM 101 Topic 11: Acids and Bases
Guided Assignment

Instructions: This assignment focuses on the definitions of acids and bases, the pH scale, and the way electrolytes behave in solution. You will practice identifying proton donors and acceptors, calculating pH, and simplifying chemical equations into net ionic forms. To get the most out of this exercise, please work through these solutions yourself on a separate sheet of paper. Section 1: Defining Acids and Bases
Problem 1: Entry Level (Arrhenius Definition) Is KOH an Arrhenius acid or an Arrhenius base? Explain why.
Identify: KOH dissociates in water to produce K+ and OH-. Classify: A substance that increases the concentration of OH- ions in water is an Arrhenius base.
Result: Arrhenius base.
Problem 2: Medium Level (Bronsted-Lowry Identification) In the reaction HF + H2O ⇌ F- + H3O+, identify the Bronsted-Lowry acid and base.
Identify: HF becomes F- (lost an H+). H2O becomes H3O+ (gained an H+). Classify: The proton (H+) donor is the acid; the proton acceptor is the base.
Result: Acid: HF; Base: H2O.
Problem 3: Difficult Level (Broader Definitions) Why is the Bronsted-Lowry definition considered "broader" than the Arrhenius definition?
Analysis: The Arrhenius definition requires the reaction to happen in water and produce H+ or OH-. The Bronsted-Lowry definition focuses only on the transfer of a proton (H+), which can happen in any solvent or even in the gas phase.
Result: It covers reactions that do not involve water or the production of hydroxide ions.
Section 2: Water, Ionization, and pH
Problem 4: Entry Level (pH Scale Basics) A solution has a pH of 3. Is it acidic, basic, or neutral?
Identify: pH = 3. Classify: pH < 7 is acidic; pH = 7 is neutral; pH > 7 is basic.
Result: Acidic.
Problem 5: Medium Level (Calculating pH) Calculate the pH of a solution where [H+] = 1.0 × 10-9 M.
Given: [H+] = 1.0 × 10-9 M. Bridge: pH = -&log[H+]. Calculate: pH = -&log(1.0 × 10-9) = -(-9) = 9. Check: pH 9 is basic, which matches a very low hydrogen ion concentration.
Result: pH = 9.
Problem 6: Difficult Level (The Logarithmic Scale) How many times more acidic is a solution with pH 2 compared to a solution with pH 4?
Identify: Difference in pH = 4 - 2 = 2 units. Classify: The pH scale is logarithmic (base 10). Each whole unit is a 10-fold change. Calculate: 102 = 100.
Result: The pH 2 solution is 100 times more acidic.
Section 3: Electrolytes & Net Ionic Equations
Problem 7: Entry Level (Strong vs. Weak) Which of the following is a strong electrolyte: HCl (hydrochloric acid) or CH3COOH (acetic acid)?
Identify: HCl is a strong acid; CH3COOH is a weak acid. Classify: Strong electrolytes ionize completely; weak electrolytes ionize partially.
Result: HCl.
Problem 8: Medium Level (Finding Spectators) In the reaction Ba(NO3)2(aq) + Na2SO4(aq) → BaSO4(s) + 2NaNO3(aq), identify the spectator ions.
Identify: Dissolve strong electrolytes: Ba2+, NO3-, Na+, SO42-. Compare: Na+ and NO3- appear as ions on both the reactant and product sides.
Result: Na+ and NO3-.
Problem 9: Difficult Level (Writing the Net Ionic Equation) Write the net ionic equation for the reaction between aqueous silver nitrate (AgNO3) and aqueous potassium chloride (KCl).
Step 1 (Full): AgNO3(aq) + KCl(aq) → AgCl(s) + KNO3(aq). Step 2 (Ionic): Ag+(aq) + NO3-(aq) + K+(aq) + Cl-(aq) → AgCl(s) + K+(aq) + NO3-(aq). Step 3 (Net): Cancel K+ and NO3-.
Result: Ag+(aq) + Cl-(aq) → AgCl(s).
Section 4: Clinical Applications
Problem 10: Entry Level (Blood pH) If a patient's blood pH drops to 7.2, is this considered acidosis or alkalosis?
Identify: Normal range is 7.35 - 7.45. Classify: pH below normal range is acidosis.
Result: Acidosis.
Problem 11: Medium Level (Antacid Action) Explain the chemistry of how an antacid like NaHCO3 (sodium bicarbonate) works to relieve heartburn.
Identify: Heartburn is caused by excess HCl (strong acid) in the stomach. Classify: NaHCO3 is a base. Explain: A neutralization reaction occurs where the base reacts with the acid to produce water and a salt, lowering the acidity.
Result: It neutralizes HCl through an acid-base reaction.
Problem 12: Difficult Level (Strong Electrolyte Impact) Why is sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3) useful for blood pH stability?
Identify: NaHCO3 is a strong electrolyte. Analysis: Because it dissociates completely, it provides a readily available source of bicarbonate ions (HCO3-) which can act as a buffer to neutralize excess H+ in the blood.
Result: Its complete dissociation ensures a constant supply of ions to stabilize pH.
