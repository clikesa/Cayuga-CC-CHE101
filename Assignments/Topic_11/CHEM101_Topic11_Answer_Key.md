# CHEM 101 Topic 11: Acids and Bases — Answer Key

---

### Section 1: Defining Acids and Bases

**Problem 1:**
- **Answer:** Arrhenius base.
- **Reasoning:** $\text{NaOH}$ dissociates in water to produce $\text{OH}^-$ ions.

**Problem 2:**
- **Acid:** $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (donates proton to $\text{NH}_3$).
- **Base:** $\text{NH}_3$ (accepts proton from $\text{H}_2\text{O}$).

**Problem 3:**
- **Answer:** Arrhenius acids always donate protons in water, fitting the BL definition. However, some BL acids donate protons in non-aqueous environments or don't specifically produce $\text{H}^+$ in water, failing the Arrhenius definition.

### Section 2: Water, Ionization, and pH

**Problem 4:**
- **Answer:** Basic (or Alkaline).
- **Reasoning:** $\text{pH} > 7$.

**Problem 5:**
- **Answer:** $\text{pH} \approx 2.60$.
- **Reasoning:** $\text{pH} = -\log(2.5 \times 10^{-3}) = -(-2.60)$.

**Problem 6:**
- **Answer:** 100 times more concentrated.
- **Reasoning:** Difference is 2 $\text{pH}$ units; $10^2 = 100$.

### Section 3: Electrolytes & Net Ionic Equations

**Problem 7:**
- $\text{HNO}_3$: Strong electrolyte.
- $\text{HF}$: Weak electrolyte.

**Problem 8:**
- **Answer:** $\text{K}^+$ and $\text{Cl}^-$.
- **Reasoning:** They remain ions on both sides of the equation.

**Problem 9:**
- **Full Ionic:** $\text{Ba}^{2+} + 2\text{NO}_3^- + 2\text{Na}^+ + \text{SO}_4^{2-} \rightarrow \text{BaSO}_4(\text{s}) + 2\text{Na}^+ + 2\text{NO}_3^-$.
- **Net Ionic:** $\text{Ba}^{2+}(\text{aq}) + \text{SO}_4^{2-}(\text{aq}) \rightarrow \text{BaSO}_4(\text{s})$.

### Section 4: Clinical Applications

**Problem 10:**
- **Answer:** Acidosis.
- **Reasoning:** $7.30$ is below the normal range of $7.35 - 7.45$.

**Problem 11:**
- **Answer:** The base in the antacid reacts with $\text{HCl}$ in a neutralization reaction, producing water and a salt, which reduces the concentration of $\text{H}^+$ ions.

**Problem 12:**
- **Answer:** As a strong electrolyte, $\text{NaHCO}_3$ dissociates completely, providing $\text{HCO}_3^-$ ions that neutralize excess $\text{H}^+$ in the blood, thereby increasing the $\text{pH}$.
