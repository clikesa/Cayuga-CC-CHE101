### Section 1: Defining Acids and Bases
*Reference: Slides 5-7*

**Problem 1: Entry Level (Arrhenius Definition)**
Is $\text{KOH}$ an Arrhenius acid or an Arrhenius base? Explain why.
- **Identify:** $\text{KOH}$ dissociates in water to produce $\text{K}^+$ and $\text{OH}^-$.
- **Classify:** A substance that increases the concentration of $\text{OH}^-$ ions in water is an Arrhenius base.
- **Result:** Arrhenius base.

**Problem 2: Medium Level (Bronsted-Lowry Identification)**
In the reaction $\text{HF} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{F}^- + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+$, identify the Bronsted-Lowry acid and base.
- **Identify:** $\text{HF}$ becomes $\text{F}^-$ (lost an $\text{H}^+$). $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ becomes $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ (gained an $\text{H}^+$).
- **Classify:** The proton ($\text{H}^+$) donor is the acid; the proton acceptor is the base.
- **Result:** Acid: $\text{HF}$; Base: $\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

**Problem 3: Difficult Level (Broader Definitions)**
Why is the Bronsted-Lowry definition considered "broader" than the Arrhenius definition?
- **Analysis:** The Arrhenius definition requires the reaction to happen in water and produce $\text{H}^+$ or $\text{OH}^-$. The Bronsted-Lowry definition focuses only on the transfer of a proton ($\text{H}^+$), which can happen in any solvent or even in the gas phase.
- **Result:** It covers reactions that do not involve water or the production of hydroxide ions.

### Section 2: Water, Ionization, and pH
*Reference: Slides 9-11*

**Problem 4: Entry Level (pH Scale Basics)**
A solution has a $\text{pH}$ of 3. Is it acidic, basic, or neutral?
- **Identify:** $\text{pH} = 3$.
- **Classify:** $\text{pH} < 7$ is acidic; $\text{pH} = 7$ is neutral; $\text{pH} > 7$ is basic.
- **Result:** Acidic.

**Problem 5: Medium Level (Calculating pH)**
Calculate the $\text{pH}$ of a solution where $[\text{H}^+] = 1.0 \times 10^{-9} \text{ M}$.
- **Given:** $[\text{H}^+] = 1.0 \times 10^{-9} \text{ M}$.
- **Bridge:** $\text{pH} = -\log[\text{H}^+]$.
- **Calculate:** $\text{pH} = -\log(1.0 \times 10^{-9}) = -(-9) = 9$.
- **Check:** $\text{pH } 9$ is basic, which matches a very low hydrogen ion concentration.
- **Result:** $\text{pH} = 9$.

**Problem 6: Difficult Level (The Logarithmic Scale)**
How many times more acidic is a solution with $\text{pH } 2$ compared to a solution with $\text{pH } 4$?
- **Identify:** Difference in $\text{pH} = 4 - 2 = 2$ units.
- **Classify:** The $\text{pH}$ scale is logarithmic (base 10). Each whole unit is a 10-fold change.
- **Calculate:** $10^2 = 100$.
- **Result:** The $\text{pH } 2$ solution is 100 times more acidic.

### Section 3: Electrolytes & Net Ionic Equations
*Reference: Slides 13-16*

**Problem 7: Entry Level (Strong vs. Weak)**
Which of the following is a strong electrolyte: $\text{HCl}$ (hydrochloric acid) or $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ (acetic acid)?
- **Identify:** $\text{HCl}$ is a strong acid; $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ is a weak acid.
- **Classify:** Strong electrolytes ionize completely; weak electrolytes ionize partially.
- **Result:** $\text{HCl}$.

**Problem 8: Medium Level (Finding Spectators)**
In the reaction $\text{Ba}(\text{NO}_3)_2(\text{aq}) + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4(\text{aq}) \rightarrow \text{BaSO}_4(\text{s}) + 2\text{NaNO}_3(\text{aq})$, identify the spectator ions.
- **Identify:** Dissolve strong electrolytes: $\text{Ba}^{2+}, \text{NO}_3^-, \text{Na}^+, \text{SO}_4^{2-}$.
- **Compare:** $\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{NO}_3^-$ appear as ions on both the reactant and product sides.
- **Result:** $\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{NO}_3^-$.

**Problem 9: Difficult Level (Writing the Net Ionic Equation)**
Write the net ionic equation for the reaction between aqueous silver nitrate ($\text{AgNO}_3$) and aqueous potassium chloride ($\text{KCl}$).
- **Step 1 (Full):** $\text{AgNO}_3(\text{aq}) + \text{KCl}(\text{aq}) \rightarrow \text{AgCl}(\text{s}) + \text{KNO}_3(\text{aq})$.
- **Step 2 (Ionic):** $\text{Ag}^+(\text{aq}) + \text{NO}_3^-(\text{aq}) + \text{K}^+(\text{aq}) + \text{Cl}^-(\text{aq}) \rightarrow \text{AgCl}(\text{s}) + \text{K}^+(\text{aq}) + \text{NO}_3^-(\text{aq})$.
- **Step 3 (Net):** Cancel $\text{K}^+$ and $\text{NO}_3^-$.
- **Result:** $\text{Ag}^+(\text{aq}) + \text{Cl}^-(\text{aq}) \rightarrow \text{AgCl}(\text{s})$.

### Section 4: Clinical Applications
*Reference: Slide 3*

**Problem 10: Entry Level (Blood pH)**
If a patient's blood $\text{pH}$ drops to 7.2, is this considered acidosis or alkalosis?
- **Identify:** Normal range is $7.35 - 7.45$.
- **Classify:** $\text{pH}$ below normal range is acidosis.
- **Result:** Acidosis.

**Problem 11: Medium Level (Antacid Action)**
Explain the chemistry of how an antacid like $\text{NaHCO}_3$ (sodium bicarbonate) works to relieve heartburn.
- **Identify:** Heartburn is caused by excess $\text{HCl}$ (strong acid) in the stomach.
- **Classify:** $\text{NaHCO}_3$ is a base.
- **Explain:** A neutralization reaction occurs where the base reacts with the acid to produce water and a salt, lowering the acidity.
- **Result:** It neutralizes $\text{HCl}$ through an acid-base reaction.

**Problem 12: Difficult Level (Strong Electrolyte Impact)**
Why is sodium bicarbonate ($\text{NaHCO}_3$) useful for blood pH stability?
- **Identify:** $\text{NaHCO}_3$ is a strong electrolyte.
- **Analysis:** Because it dissociates completely, it provides a readily available source of bicarbonate ions ($\text{HCO}_3^-$) which can act as a buffer to neutralize excess $\text{H}^+$ in the blood.
- **Result:** Its complete dissociation ensures a constant supply of ions to stabilize $\text{pH}$.
