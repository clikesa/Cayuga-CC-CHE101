CHEM 101 Topic 10: Chemical Bonds
Guided Assignment

Instructions: This assignment explores how atoms bond to form compounds and the 3D shapes those molecules take. You will practice distinguishing between ionic and covalent bonds, drawing electron dot structures, and predicting molecular geometry using VSEPR theory. To get the most out of this exercise, please work through these solutions yourself on a separate sheet of paper. Practice using the analysis steps (Identify → Classify → Draw/Predict → Check) for each problem to build your muscle memory before attempting the graded problem set for Topic 10.
Section 1: Ionic and Covalent Bonds
Problem 1: Entry Level (Bond Identification) Determine if the bond in NaCl is ionic or covalent.
Identify: Na is a metal; Cl is a nonmetal. Classify: Bonding between a metal and a nonmetal involves electron transfer.
Result: Ionic bond.
Problem 2: Medium Level (Bond Characteristics) Contrast the melting points and electrical conductivity of an ionic compound (like MgO) versus a covalent molecule (like CO2).
Identify: MgO is ionic (metal + nonmetal); CO2 is covalent (nonmetal + nonmetal). Compare (Melting Point): Ionic bonds form rigid lattices → High melting point. Covalent molecules are held by weaker intermolecular forces → Low to moderate melting point. Compare (Conductivity): MgO conducts electricity when dissolved (ions separate). CO2 generally does not conduct.
Result: MgO has a higher melting point and conducts when dissolved; CO2 has a lower melting point and does not conduct.
Problem 3: Difficult Level (Structure Difference) Explain why NaCl forms a crystal lattice while H2O forms individual molecules.
Analysis: NaCl is held by omnidirectional electrostatic attraction between Na+ and Cl- ions, creating a repeating 3D grid (lattice). H2O is held by specific shared electron pairs between O and H, creating a distinct, limited unit (molecule).
Result: Ionic bonds create lattices; covalent bonds create molecules.
Section 2: Electron Dot Structures
Problem 4: Entry Level (Valence Count) How many total valence electrons are available to draw the structure for CH4 (Methane)?
Identify: C (Group 14) = 4 valence electrons. H (Group 1) = 1 valence electron × 4 atoms = 4 electrons. Calculate: 4 + 4 = 8 total valence electrons.
Result: 8 valence electrons.
Problem 5: Medium Level (Step-by-Step Drawing) Draw the electron dot structure for NF3.
Step 1 (Count): N(5) + 3 × F(7) = 5 + 21 = 26 total electrons. Step 2 (Arrange): N is the center atom, F atoms surround it. Step 3 (Bond): Place one pair between N and each F. (3 bonds × 2e- = 6e- used). Step 4 (Fill): Distribute remaining 20e-. Give each F three lone pairs (6 each × 3 = 18e-). Place the last pair on N.
Result: N in center with one lone pair and three single bonds to F atoms.
Problem 6: Difficult Level (Multiple Bonds) Draw the electron dot structure for CO2.
Step 1 (Count): C(4) + 2 × O(6) = 16 total electrons. Step 2 (Arrange): C is the center atom. Step 3 (Bond): Place single bonds O-C-O. (4 electrons used, 12 remaining). Step 4 (Fill): Fill O octets. (6 each × 2 = 12e- used). Check Octets: O atoms are full, but C only has 4 electrons. Move one lone pair from each O to form double bonds.
Result: O=C=O with two lone pairs on each Oxygen.
Section 3: Molecular Geometry (VSEPR)
Problem 7: Entry Level (Linear Shape) What is the molecular geometry of BeCl2 if the central Be atom has 2 bonding pairs and 0 lone pairs?
Identify: 2 electron groups. VSEPR Rule: Two groups repel to opposite sides (180°).
Result: Linear.
Problem 8: Medium Level (Bent Shape) Using the structure of H2O (2 bonding pairs, 2 lone pairs on O), explain why the shape is "Bent" rather than "Linear."
Identify: 4 total electron groups around O. VSEPR Rule: Lone pairs occupy more space and repel bonding pairs more strongly. Explain: The two lone pairs push the O-H bonds closer together, creating an angle of approximately 104.5°.
Result: Bent.
Problem 9: Difficult Level (Tetrahedral Prediction) Predict the shape of CCl4.
Identify: C is the center; it has 4 bonding pairs and 0 lone pairs. VSEPR Rule: 4 groups arrange themselves as far apart as possible in 3D space.
Result: Tetrahedral.
Section 4: Coordinate Bonds & Polarity
Problem 10: Entry Level (Coordinate Bonds) In hemoglobin, how does the iron (Fe) atom bond to oxygen?
Identify: The oxygen molecule donates both electrons of the shared pair. Classify: This "one-sided" sharing is a coordinate covalent bond.
Result: Coordinate covalent bond.
Problem 11: Medium Level (Bond Polarity) Is the C-O bond in CO2 polar or non-polar? Justify using electronegativity.
Identify: Oxygen is significantly more electronegative than Carbon. Classify: Electrons are shared unequally, pulled toward the Oxygen.
Result: Polar covalent bond.
Problem 12: Difficult Level (Molecular Polarity) Even though the C-O bonds in CO2 are polar, the entire CO2 molecule is non-polar. Explain why.
Identify: CO2 is linear (O=C=O). Analyze: The two polar bonds point in exactly opposite directions.
Result: The bond polarities cancel each other out, resulting in a non-polar molecule.
