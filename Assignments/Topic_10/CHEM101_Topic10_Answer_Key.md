### Section 1: Ionic and Covalent Bonds

**Problem 1:**
- **Answer:** Ionic bond.
- **Reasoning:** $\text{K}$ is a metal and $\text{O}$ is a nonmetal $\rightarrow$ electron transfer.

**Problem 2:**
- **Answer:** $\text{NaF}$ has a much higher melting point and is more soluble in water (as an electrolyte) than $\text{NH}_3$.
- **Reasoning:** Ionic bonds (in $\text{NaF}$) are stronger and more rigid than the intermolecular forces between covalent molecules (in $\text{NH}_3$).

**Problem 3:**
- **Answer:** A molecule is a distinct, limited group of atoms held by covalent bonds; a crystal lattice is a repeating 3D grid of ions held by ionic bonds. Ionic bonds form the lattice.

### Section 2: Electron Dot Structures

**Problem 4:**
- **Answer:** 26 valence electrons.
- **Reasoning:** $\text{P}(5) + 3 \times \text{Cl}(7) = 5 + 21 = 26$.

**Problem 5:**
- **Answer:** $\text{S}$ in center, two single bonds to $\text{H}$ atoms, and two lone pairs on $\text{S}$.
- **Reasoning:** Total electrons = 8. 2 used for $\text{S}-\text{H}$ bonds, 4 used for lone pairs on $\text{S}$.

**Problem 6:**
- **Answer:** $\text{H}-\text{C}\equiv\text{N}\text{:}$
- **Reasoning:** Total electrons = 10. Single bond $\text{H}-\text{C}$, triple bond $\text{C}\equiv\text{N}$, and one lone pair on $\text{N}$ to satisfy octets.

### Section 3: Molecular Geometry (VSEPR)

**Problem 7:**
- **Answer:** Linear.
- **Reasoning:** 2 electron groups repel to $180^\circ$.

**Problem 8:**
- **Answer:** Trigonal Pyramidal.
- **Reasoning:** 3 bonding pairs and 1 lone pair push the bonds downward.

**Problem 9:**
- **Answer:** Tetrahedral.
- **Reasoning:** 4 bonding pairs arrange themselves in a 3D tetrahedral shape to minimize repulsion.

---

### Section 4: Coordinate Bonds & Polarity

**Problem 10:**
- **Answer:** A coordinate covalent bond is one where one atom provides both electrons for the shared pair. A standard covalent bond involves each atom contributing one electron.

**Problem 11:**
- **Answer:** Polar covalent.
- **Reasoning:** Nitrogen is significantly more electronegative than Hydrogen, leading to unequal sharing of electrons.

**Problem 12:**
- **Answer:** Polar.
- **Reasoning:** Because the molecule is "Bent", the polar $\text{O}-\text{H}$ bonds do not cancel each other out, resulting in a net dipole moment toward the oxygen.
