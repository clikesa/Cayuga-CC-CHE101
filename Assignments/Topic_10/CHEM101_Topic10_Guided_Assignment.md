### Section 1: Ionic and Covalent Bonds
*Reference: Slides 5-7*

**Problem 1: Entry Level (Bond Identification)**
Determine if the bond in $\text{NaCl}$ is ionic or covalent.
- **Identify:** $\text{Na}$ is a metal; $\text{Cl}$ is a nonmetal.
- **Classify:** Bonding between a metal and a nonmetal involves electron transfer.
- **Result:** Ionic bond.

**Problem 2: Medium Level (Bond Characteristics)**
Contrast the melting points and electrical conductivity of an ionic compound (like $\text{MgO}$) versus a covalent molecule (like $\text{CO}_2$).
- **Identify:** $\text{MgO}$ is ionic (metal + nonmetal); $\text{CO}_2$ is covalent (nonmetal + nonmetal).
- **Compare (Melting Point):** Ionic bonds form rigid lattices $\rightarrow$ High melting point. Covalent molecules are held by weaker intermolecular forces $\rightarrow$ Low to moderate melting point.
- **Compare (Conductivity):** $\text{MgO}$ conducts electricity when dissolved (ions separate). $\text{CO}_2$ generally does not conduct.
- **Result:** $\text{MgO}$ has a higher melting point and conducts when dissolved; $\text{CO}_2$ has a lower melting point and does not conduct.

**Problem 3: Difficult Level (Structure Difference)**
Explain why $\text{NaCl}$ forms a crystal lattice while $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ forms individual molecules.
- **Analysis:** $\text{NaCl}$ is held by omnidirectional electrostatic attraction between $\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{Cl}^-$ ions, creating a repeating 3D grid (lattice). $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is held by specific shared electron pairs between $\text{O}$ and $\text{H}$, creating a distinct, limited unit (molecule).
- **Result:** Ionic bonds create lattices; covalent bonds create molecules.

### Section 2: Electron Dot Structures
*Reference: Slides 9-10*

**Problem 4: Entry Level (Valence Count)**
How many total valence electrons are available to draw the structure for $\text{CH}_4$ (Methane)?
- **Identify:** $\text{C}$ (Group 14) = 4 valence electrons. $\text{H}$ (Group 1) = 1 valence electron $\times 4$ atoms = 4 electrons.
- **Calculate:** $4 + 4 = 8$ total valence electrons.
- **Result:** 8 valence electrons.

**Problem 5: Medium Level (Step-by-Step Drawing)**
Draw the electron dot structure for $\text{NF}_3$.
- **Step 1 (Count):** $\text{N}(5) + 3 \times \text{F}(7) = 5 + 21 = 26$ total electrons.
- **Step 2 (Arrange):** $\text{N}$ is the center atom, $\text{F}$ atoms surround it.
- **Step 3 (Bond):** Place one pair between $\text{N}$ and each $\text{F}$. (3 bonds $\times 2\text{e}^- = 6\text{e}^-$ used).
- **Step 4 (Fill):** Distribute remaining $20\text{e}^-$. Give each $\text{F}$ three lone pairs (6 each $\times 3 = 18\text{e}^-$). Place the last pair on $\text{N}$.
- **Result:** $\text{N}$ in center with one lone pair and three single bonds to $\text{F}$ atoms.

**Problem 6: Difficult Level (Multiple Bonds)**
Draw the electron dot structure for $\text{CO}_2$.
- **Step 1 (Count):** $\text{C}(4) + 2 \times \text{O}(6) = 16$ total electrons.
- **Step 2 (Arrange):** $\text{C}$ is the center atom.
- **Step 3 (Bond):** Place single bonds $\text{O}-\text{C}-\text{O}$. (4 electrons used, 12 remaining).
- **Step 4 (Fill):** Fill $\text{O}$ octets. (6 each $\times 2 = 12\text{e}^-$ used).
- **Check Octets:** $\text{O}$ atoms are full, but $\text{C}$ only has 4 electrons. Move one lone pair from each $\text{O}$ to form double bonds.
- **Result:** $\text{O}=\text{C}=\text{O}$ with two lone pairs on each Oxygen.

### Section 3: Molecular Geometry (VSEPR)
*Reference: Slides 11-12*

**Problem 7: Entry Level (Linear Shape)**
What is the molecular geometry of $\text{BeCl}_2$ if the central $\text{Be}$ atom has 2 bonding pairs and 0 lone pairs?
- **Identify:** 2 electron groups.
- **VSEPR Rule:** Two groups repel to opposite sides ($180^\circ$).
- **Result:** Linear.

**Problem 8: Medium Level (Bent Shape)**
Using the structure of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (2 bonding pairs, 2 lone pairs on $\text{O}$), explain why the shape is "Bent" rather than "Linear."
- **Identify:** 4 total electron groups around $\text{O}$.
- **VSEPR Rule:** Lone pairs occupy more space and repel bonding pairs more strongly.
- **Explain:** The two lone pairs push the $\text{O}-\text{H}$ bonds closer together, creating an angle of approximately $104.5^\circ$.
- **Result:** Bent.

**Problem 9: Difficult Level (Tetrahedral Prediction)**
Predict the shape of $\text{CCl}_4$.
- **Identify:** $\text{C}$ is the center; it has 4 bonding pairs and 0 lone pairs.
- **VSEPR Rule:** 4 groups arrange themselves as far apart as possible in 3D space.
- **Result:** Tetrahedral.

### Section 4: Coordinate Bonds & Polarity
*Reference: Slides 14-16*

**Problem 10: Entry Level (Coordinate Bonds)**
In hemoglobin, how does the iron ($\text{Fe}$) atom bond to oxygen?
- **Identify:** The oxygen molecule donates both electrons of the shared pair.
- **Classify:** This "one-sided" sharing is a coordinate covalent bond.
- **Result:** Coordinate covalent bond.

**Problem 11: Medium Level (Bond Polarity)**
Is the $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bond in $\text{CO}_2$ polar or non-polar? Justify using electronegativity.
- **Identify:** Oxygen is significantly more electronegative than Carbon.
- **Classify:** Electrons are shared unequally, pulled toward the Oxygen.
- **Result:** Polar covalent bond.

**Problem 12: Difficult Level (Molecular Polarity)**
Even though the $\text{C}-\text{O}$ bonds in $\text{CO}_2$ are polar, the entire $\text{CO}_2$ molecule is non-polar. Explain why.
- **Identify:** $\text{CO}_2$ is linear ($\text{O}=\text{C}=\text{O}$).
- **Analyze:** The two polar bonds point in exactly opposite directions.
- **Result:** The bond polarities cancel each other out, resulting in a non-polar molecule.
