### Section 1: What Makes Chemistry "Organic"?
*Reference: Slides 5-6*

**Problem 1: Entry Level (The Carbon Rule)**
Why is carbon the central element of organic chemistry? What is its most unique bonding property?
- **Identify:** Carbon is in Group 14 and has 4 valence electrons.
- **Explain:** This allows carbon to form 4 covalent bonds. Most importantly, carbon can bond to other carbon atoms, forming long chains, rings, and branches.
- **Result:** Its ability to form four bonds and bond to itself creates immense structural diversity.

**Problem 2: Medium Level (Organic vs. Inorganic)**
Given the following compounds, classify them as organic or inorganic: $\text{CH}_3\text{OH}$ (methanol), $\text{NaCl}$ (table salt), $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$ (glucose), and $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ (sulfuric acid).
- **Identify:** $\text{CH}_3\text{OH}$ and $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$ contain carbon-hydrogen bonds. $\text{NaCl}$ and $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ do not.
- **Classify:** Organic compounds are carbon-based. Inorganic compounds are generally not.
- **Result:** Organic: $\text{CH}_3\text{OH}, \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$; Inorganic: $\text{NaCl}, \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$.

**Problem 3: Difficult Level (Bonding Comparison)**
Compare the typical bonding and physical states of organic molecules versus inorganic salts.
- **Analysis:** Organic molecules are held by covalent bonds and often exist as small, discrete molecules (many are liquids or gases). Inorganic salts are held by ionic bonds in repeating 3D lattices, making them hard, crystalline solids with high melting points.
- **Result:** Organic $\rightarrow$ Covalent/Molecules; Inorganic $\rightarrow$ Ionic/Lattices.

### Section 2: Hydrocarbons
*Reference: Slides 8-9*

**Problem 4: Entry Level (Identifying Alkanes)**
A hydrocarbon has the formula $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8$. Is this an alkane, alkene, or alkyne?
- **Identify:** Formula $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8$ fits the $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$ pattern ($2 \times 3 + 2 = 8$).
- **Classify:** Compounds with only single $\text{C}-\text{C}$ bonds are alkanes.
- **Result:** Alkane.

**Problem 5: Medium Level (Naming Alkanes)**
What is the name of a straight-chain alkane with 4 carbon atoms?
- **Identify:** 4 carbons $\rightarrow$ prefix "but-".
- **Classify:** All single bonds $\rightarrow$ suffix "-ane".
- **Result:** Butane.

**Problem 6: Difficult Level (Formula Prediction)**
Predict the molecular formula for an alkene with 5 carbon atoms.
- **Identify:** Alkene general formula is $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$.
- **Calculate:** For $n=5$, the formula is $\text{C}_5\text{H}_{2 \times 5} = \text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}$.
- **Result:** $\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}$.

### Section 3: Functional Groups
*Reference: Slides 11-12*

**Problem 7: Entry Level (Alcohol Group)**
Identify the functional group present in ethanol ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$).
- **Identify:** The molecule ends in $-\text{OH}$.
- **Classify:** The $-\text{OH}$ group is known as the hydroxyl group.
- **Result:** Hydroxyl (alcohol).

**Problem 8: Medium Level (Carboxylic Acids)**
A drug contains the group $-\text{COOH}$. What is the name of this functional group, and what common property does it give the molecule?
- **Identify:** $-\text{COOH}$ group.
- **Classify:** This is the carboxyl group.
- **Explain:** It makes the molecule a carboxylic acid, giving it acidic properties (able to donate a proton).
- **Result:** Carboxyl group; provides acidity.

**Problem 9: Difficult Level (Medicine Link)**
Many medications contain amine groups ($-\text{NH}_2$). Based on your knowledge of nitrogen, why are amines important for drug-receptor interactions?
- **Identify:** Amines contain nitrogen.
- **Analysis:** Nitrogen often allows the molecule to act as a base or form hydrogen bonds, which is critical for "fitting" into a biological receptor (the lock-and-key model).
- **Result:** Amines provide specific polarity and bonding sites that enable target specificity in the body.

### Section 4: Isomers and Biomolecules
*Reference: Slides 14-15*

**Problem 10: Entry Level (Isomer Definition)**
Two molecules both have the formula $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6\text{O}$, but one is an alcohol and the other is an ether. What is the term for these molecules?
- **Identify:** Same molecular formula, different structure/properties.
- **Classify:** This is the definition of isomers.
- **Result:** Isomers.

**Problem 11: Medium Level (Biomolecule Identification)**
Which of the four major biomolecules are primarily used for long-term energy storage and cell membrane structure?
- **Identify:** Carbohydrates (short-term), Proteins (structure/enzymes), Nucleic Acids (genetic info), Lipids (fats).
- **Classify:** Lipids are the primary long-term energy store.
- **Result:** Lipids.

**Problem 12: Difficult Level (The Protein Link)**
Proteins are built from amino acids. Why is the specific sequence of these amino acids so critical to the protein's function?
- **Analysis:** The sequence determines how the protein folds into a 3D shape. Because function depends on shape (like an enzyme's active site), a wrong sequence leads to a wrong shape and a non-functional protein.
- **Result:** Sequence $\rightarrow$ Folding $\rightarrow$ Shape $\rightarrow$ Function.
