### Section 1: Writing and Balancing Equations
*Reference: Slides 4-7*

**Problem 1: Entry Level (The Basics)**
Identify the reactants and products in the following equation:
$2\text{H}_2(\text{g}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g}) \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l})$
- **Identify:** Substances on the left are reactants; substances on the right are products.
- **Classify:** Reactants: Hydrogen gas ($\text{H}_2$) and Oxygen gas ($\text{O}_2$). Product: Liquid water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
- **Check:** The arrow indicates the direction of the reaction (reactants $\rightarrow$ products).
- **Result:** Reactants: $\text{H}_2, \text{O}_2$; Product: $\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

**Problem 2: Medium Level (Balancing a Simple Equation)**
Balance the following equation: $\text{Mg} + \text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{MgCl}_2 + \text{H}_2$
- **Identify:** Unbalanced atoms: $\text{Mg}$ (1 vs 1), $\text{Cl}$ (1 vs 2), $\text{H}$ (1 vs 2).
- **Classify:** The $\text{Cl}$ and $\text{H}$ atoms are deficient on the reactant side.
- **Balance:** Place a coefficient of 2 in front of $\text{HCl}$.
- **Check:** Left side: $1\text{Mg}, 2\text{H}, 2\text{Cl}$. Right side: $1\text{Mg}, 2\text{H}, 2\text{Cl}$. Balanced!
- **Result:** $\text{Mg} + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{MgCl}_2 + \text{H}_2$.

**Problem 3: Difficult Level (Complex Balancing)**
Balance the following equation: $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$
- **Identify:** Most complex molecule first ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_8$).
- **Step 1 (Carbon):** 3 $\text{C}$ on left $\rightarrow$ place 3 in front of $\text{CO}_2$.
- **Step 2 (Hydrogen):** 8 $\text{H}$ on left $\rightarrow$ place 4 in front of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (since $4 \times 2 = 8$).
- **Step 3 (Oxygen):** Count oxygens on right: $(3 \times 2) + (4 \times 1) = 10$. To get 10 $\text{O}$ on left, place 5 in front of $\text{O}_2$.
- **Check:** Left: $3\text{C}, 8\text{H}, 10\text{O}$. Right: $3\text{C}, 8\text{H}, 10\text{O}$. Balanced!
- **Result:** $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8 + 5\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 3\text{CO}_2 + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

### Section 2: Types of Chemical Reactions
*Reference: Slides 8-12*

**Problem 4: Entry Level (Identifying a Pattern)**
Classify the reaction: $2\text{KClO}_3 \rightarrow 2\text{KCl} + 3\text{O}_2$
- **Identify:** One complex reactant breaking down into two simpler products.
- **Classify:** This fits the general form $\text{AB} \rightarrow \text{A} + \text{B}$.
- **Result:** Decomposition reaction.

**Problem 5: Medium Level (Predicting Combustion)**
Predict the products for the combustion of methane ($\text{CH}_4$).
- **Identify:** Hydrocarbon ($\text{CH}_4$) + Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$).
- **Classify:** Combustion reaction.
- **Predict:** Complete combustion of any hydrocarbon always yields carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) and water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
- **Result:** $\text{CH}_4 + 2\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

**Problem 6: Difficult Level (Single vs. Double Replacement)**
Contrast these two reactions:
(a) $\text{Zn} + \text{CuSO}_4 \rightarrow \text{ZnSO}_4 + \text{Cu}$
(b) $\text{AgNO}_3 + \text{NaCl} \rightarrow \text{AgCl} + \text{NaNO}_3$
- **Analysis (a):** A single element ($\text{Zn}$) swaps places with a metal in a compound ($\text{Cu}$). $\rightarrow$ Single Replacement.
- **Analysis (b):** Two compounds swap their positive ions (cations). $\rightarrow$ Double Replacement.
- **Result:** (a) is Single Replacement; (b) is Double Replacement.

### Section 3: Solubility Rules & Precipitation
*Reference: Slides 13-16*

**Problem 7: Entry Level (Using Rules)**
Will a precipitate form if you mix $\text{KNO}_3(\text{aq})$ and $\text{LiCl}(\text{aq})$?
- **Identify:** Potential products are $\text{KCl}$ and $\text{LiNO}_3$.
- **Classify:** Group 1 metals ($\text{K, Li}$) and Nitrates ($\text{NO}_3^-$) are always soluble.
- **Predict:** Both products are soluble; no solid forms.
- **Result:** No precipitate.

**Problem 8: Medium Level (Predicting the Solid)**
Predict the precipitate when mixing $\text{BaCl}_2(\text{aq})$ and $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4(\text{aq})$.
- **Identify:** Swap partners $\rightarrow$ $\text{BaSO}_4$ and $\text{NaCl}$.
- **Classify:** $\text{NaCl}$ is soluble (Group 1). $\text{BaSO}_4$ is an exception to the sulfate rule (insoluble).
- **Predict:** $\text{BaSO}_4$ will be the precipitate.
- **Result:** $\text{BaSO}_4(\text{s})$.

**Problem 9: Difficult Level (Full Prediction & Balance)**
Predict and balance the reaction between aqueous lead(II) nitrate and aqueous potassium iodide.
- **Identify:** $\text{Pb}(\text{NO}_3)_2(\text{aq}) + \text{KI}(\text{aq}) \rightarrow ?$
- **Swap Partners:** $\text{PbI}_2$ and $\text{KNO}_3$.
- **Solubility Check:** $\text{KNO}_3$ is soluble. $\text{PbI}_2$ is an exception to the iodide rule (insoluble $\rightarrow$ precipitate).
- **Balance:** $\text{Pb}(\text{NO}_3)_2 + 2\text{KI} \rightarrow \text{PbI}_2(\text{s}) + 2\text{KNO}_3(\text{aq})$.
- **Result:** $\text{Pb}(\text{NO}_3)_2(\text{aq}) + 2\text{KI}(\text{aq}) \rightarrow \text{PbI}_2(\text{s}) + 2\text{KNO}_3(\text{aq})$.

### Section 4: Clinical Applications & Synthesis
*Reference: Slide 3*

**Problem 10: Entry Level (Antacids)**
What type of reaction occurs when calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) neutralizes stomach acid ($\text{HCl}$)?
- **Identify:** $\text{CaCO}_3 + \text{HCl}$.
- **Classify:** Two compounds swapping ions.
- **Result:** Double-replacement reaction.

**Problem 11: Medium Level (Kidney Stones)**
Kidney stones often consist of calcium oxalate. Write the formula for calcium oxalate given $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$.
- **Identify:** Cation $+2$; Anion $-2$.
- **Balance:** 1:1 ratio.
- **Result:** $\text{CaC}_2\text{O}_4$.

**Problem 12: Difficult Level (Metabolism)**
Cellular respiration is compared to combustion. Write the unbalanced equation for the combustion of glucose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$).
- **Identify:** Fuel ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$) + $\text{O}_2$.
- **Predict:** Products are always $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
- **Result:** $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$.
