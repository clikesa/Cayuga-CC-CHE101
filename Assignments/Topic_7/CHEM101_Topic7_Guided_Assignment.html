CHEM 101 Topic 7: Chemical Reactions
Guided Assignment

Instructions: This assignment focuses on the language of chemical reactions: writing equations, balancing them, identifying reaction types, and predicting precipitates using solubility rules. To get the most out of this exercise, please work through these solutions yourself on a separate sheet of paper. Practice using the analysis steps (Identify → Classify → Balance/Predict → Check) for each problem to build your muscle memory before attempting the graded problem set for Topic 7.
Section 1: Writing and Balancing Equations
Problem 1: Entry Level (The Basics) Identify the reactants and products in the following equation: 2H2(g) + O2(g) → 2H2O(l)
Identify: Substances on the left are reactants; substances on the right are products. Classify: Reactants: Hydrogen gas (H2) and Oxygen gas (O2). Product: Liquid water (H2O). Check: The arrow indicates the direction of the reaction (reactants → products).
Result: Reactants: H2, O2; Product: H2O.
Problem 2: Medium Level (Balancing a Simple Equation) Balance the following equation: Mg + HCl → MgCl2 + H2
Identify: Unbalanced atoms: Mg (1 vs 1), Cl (1 vs 2), H (1 vs 2). Classify: The Cl and H atoms are deficient on the reactant side. Balance: Place a coefficient of 2 in front of HCl. Check: Left side: 1Mg, 2H, 2Cl. Right side: 1Mg, 2H, 2Cl. Balanced!
Result: Mg + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2.
Problem 3: Difficult Level (Complex Balancing) Balance the following equation: C3H8 + O2 → CO2 + H2O
Identify: Most complex molecule first (C3H8). Step 1 (Carbon): 3 C on left → place 3 in front of CO2. Step 2 (Hydrogen): 8 H on left → place 4 in front of H2O (since 4 × 2 = 8). Step 3 (Oxygen): Count oxygens on right: (3 × 2) + (4 × 1) = 10. To get 10 O on left, place 5 in front of O2. Check: Left: 3C, 8H, 10O. Right: 3C, 8H, 10O. Balanced!
Result: C3H8 + 5O2 → 3CO2 + 4H2O.
Section 2: Types of Chemical Reactions
Problem 4: Entry Level (Identifying a Pattern) Classify the reaction: 2KClO3 → 2KCl + 3O2
Identify: One complex reactant breaking down into two simpler products. Classify: This fits the general form AB → A + B.
Result: Decomposition reaction.
Problem 5: Medium Level (Predicting Combustion) Predict the products for the combustion of methane (CH4).
Identify: Hydrocarbon (CH4) + Oxygen (O2). Classify: Combustion reaction. Predict: Complete combustion of any hydrocarbon always yields carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O).
Result: CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O.
Problem 6: Difficult Level (Single vs. Double Replacement) Contrast these two reactions: (a) Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu; (b) AgNO3 + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO3
Analysis (a): A single element (Zn) swaps places with a metal in a compound (Cu). → Single Replacement. Analysis (b): Two compounds swap their positive ions (cations). → Double Replacement.
Result: (a) is Single Replacement; (b) is Double Replacement.
Section 3: Solubility Rules & Precipitation
Problem 7: Entry Level (Using Rules) Will a precipitate form if you mix KNO3(aq) and LiCl(aq)?
Identify: Potential products are KCl and LiNO3. Classify: Group 1 metals (K, Li) and Nitrates (NO3-) are always soluble. Predict: Both products are soluble; no solid forms.
Result: No precipitate.
Problem 8: Medium Level (Predicting the Solid) Predict the precipitate when mixing BaCl2(aq) and Na2SO4(aq).
Identify: Swap partners → BaSO4 and NaCl. Classify: NaCl is soluble (Group 1). BaSO4 is an exception to the sulfate rule (insoluble). Predict: BaSO4 will be the precipitate.
Result: BaSO4(s).
Problem 9: Difficult Level (Full Prediction & Balance) Predict and balance the reaction between aqueous lead(II) nitrate and aqueous potassium iodide.
Identify: Pb(NO3)2(aq) + KI(aq) → ? Swap Partners: PbI2 and KNO3. Solubility Check: KNO3 is soluble. PbI2 is an exception to the iodide rule (insoluble → precipitate). Balance: Pb(NO3)2 + 2KI → PbI2(s) + 2KNO3(aq).
Result: Pb(NO3)2(aq) + 2KI(aq) → PbI2(s) + 2KNO3(aq).
Section 4: Clinical Applications & Synthesis
Problem 10: Entry Level (Antacids) What type of reaction occurs when calcium carbonate (CaCO3) neutralizes stomach acid (HCl)?
Identify: CaCO3 + HCl. Classify: Two compounds swapping ions.
Result: Double-replacement reaction.
Problem 11: Medium Level (Kidney Stones) Kidney stones often consist of calcium oxalate. Write the formula for calcium oxalate given Ca2+ and C2O42-.
Identify: Cation +2; Anion -2. Balance: 1:1 ratio.
Result: CaC2O4.
Problem 12: Difficult Level (Metabolism) Cellular respiration is compared to combustion. Write the unbalanced equation for the combustion of glucose (C6H12O6).
Identify: Fuel (C6H12O6) + O2. Predict: Products are always CO2 and H2O.
Result: C6H12O6 + O2 → CO2 + H2O.
