CHEM 101 Topic 9: Stoichiometry Calculations
Guided Assignment

Instructions: This assignment focuses on stoichiometry: the quantitative relationship between reactants and products in a chemical reaction. You will practice the "Stoichiometry Roadmap" to convert between masses, moles, and volumes of solutions. To get the most out of this exercise, please work through these solutions yourself on a separate sheet of paper. Practice using the analysis steps (Given → Bridge → Calculate → Check) for each problem to build your muscle memory before attempting the graded problem set for Topic 9.
Section 1: Mole-Mole Calculations
Problem 1: Entry Level (Basic Ratio) Using the balanced equation N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3, how many moles of NH3 are produced from 2.50 mol of N2?
Given: 2.50 mol N2. Bridge: Mole ratio from coefficients: 1 mol N2 → 2 mol NH3. Calculate: 2.50 mol N2 × (2 mol NH3 / 1 mol N2) = 5.00 mol NH3. Check: The ratio is 1:2, so the product should be double the reactant.
Result: 5.00 mol NH3.
Problem 2: Medium Level (Inverse Ratio) Using the same equation (N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3), how many moles of H2 are required to produce 4.00 mol of NH3?
Given: 4.00 mol NH3. Bridge: Mole ratio: 2 mol NH3 → 3 mol H2. Calculate: 4.00 mol NH3 × (3 mol H2 / 2 mol NH3) = 6.00 mol H2. Check: The ratio is 2:3. 4 mol is double the coefficient of NH3, so we need double the coefficient of H2.
Result: 6.00 mol H2.
Problem 3: Difficult Level (Identifying the Ratio) For the reaction 4Al + 3O2 → 2Al2O3, how many moles of O2 are needed to react completely with 1.25 mol of Al?
Given: 1.25 mol Al. Bridge: Mole ratio: 4 mol Al → 3 mol O2. Calculate: 1.25 mol Al × (3 mol O2 / 4 mol Al) = 0.938 mol O2. Check: The ratio is 4:3, so we need slightly less O2 than we have Al.
Result: 0.938 mol O2.
Section 2: Mole-Mass Calculations
Problem 4: Entry Level (Mass to Mass) Using N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3, how many grams of NH3 are produced from 5.00 g of N2? (Molar masses: N2 = 28.02 g/mol, NH3 = 17.03 g/mol)
Step 1 (Grams → Moles): 5.00 g N2 ÷ 28.02 g/mol = 0.178 mol N2. Step 2 (Mole Ratio): 0.178 mol N2 × (2 mol NH3 / 1 mol N2) = 0.357 mol NH3. Step 3 (Moles → Grams): 0.357 mol NH3 × 17.03 g/mol = 6.08 g.
Result: 6.08 g NH3.
Problem 5: Medium Level (Reverse Roadmap) How many grams of Fe are needed to produce 20.0 g of Fe2O3 in the reaction 4Fe + 3O2 → 2Fe2O3? (Molar masses: Fe = 55.85 g/mol, Fe2O3 = 159.69 g/mol)
Step 1 (Grams → Moles): 20.0 g Fe2O3 ÷ 159.69 g/mol = 0.125 mol Fe2O3. Step 2 (Mole Ratio): 0.125 mol Fe2O3 × (4 mol Fe / 2 mol Fe2O3) = 0.250 mol Fe. Step 3 (Moles → Grams): 0.250 mol Fe × 55.85 g/mol = 13.96 g.
Result: 13.96 g Fe.
Problem 6: Difficult Level (Multi-Step Synthesis) In the reaction C3H8 + 5O2 → 3CO2 + 4H2O, if 100 g of C3H8 is burned, how many grams of H2O are produced? (Molar masses: C3H8 = 44.11 g/mol, H2O = 18.02 g/mol)
Step 1: 100 g C3H8 ÷ 44.11 g/mol = 2.267 mol C3H8. Step 2: 2.267 mol C3H8 × (4 mol H2O / 1 mol C3H8) = 9.068 mol H2O. Step 3: 9.068 mol H2O × 18.02 g/mol = 163.4 g.
Result: 163.4 g H2O.
Section 3: Molar Solutions
Problem 7: Entry Level (Volume to Moles) How many moles of HCl are in 250 mL of a 0.500 M solution?
Given: V = 0.250 L; M = 0.500 mol/L. Bridge: moles = Molarity × Volume. Calculate: 0.500 mol/L × 0.250 L = 0.125 mol.
Result: 0.125 mol HCl.
Problem 8: Medium Level (Molarity to Mass) What mass of NaCl is needed to prepare 500 mL of a 0.150 M solution? (Molar mass NaCl = 58.44 g/mol)
Step 1 (Volume → Moles): 0.150 mol/L × 0.500 L = 0.075 mol NaCl. Step 2 (Moles → Grams): 0.075 mol × 58.44 g/mol = 4.38 g.
Result: 4.38 g NaCl.
Problem 9: Difficult Level (Solution Stoichiometry) 50.0 mL of 0.100 M AgNO3 reacts with NaCl. How many grams of AgCl precipitate will form? (AgNO3 + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO3; Molar mass AgCl = 143.32 g/mol)
Step 1 (Volume → Moles): 0.100 mol/L × 0.0500 L = 0.00500 mol AgNO3. Step 2 (Mole Ratio): Ratio is 1:1 → 0.00500 mol AgCl. Step 3 (Moles → Grams): 0.00500 mol × 143.32 g/mol = 0.717 g.
Result: 0.717 g AgCl.
Section 4: Percent Yield
Problem 10: Entry Level (Basic Yield) If a reaction has a theoretical yield of 10.0 g but only 8.5 g is collected, what is the percent yield?
Given: Actual = 8.5 g; Theoretical = 10.0 g. Bridge: Percent Yield = (Actual / Theoretical) × 100%. Calculate: (8.5 ÷ 10.0) × 100% = 85%.
Result: 85%.
Problem 11: Medium Level (Finding Actual Yield) A reaction with a theoretical yield of 25.0 g has a percent yield of 72%. What was the actual yield?
Given: Theoretical = 25.0 g; Yield = 0.72. Calculate: Actual = Theoretical × Yield = 25.0 g × 0.72 = 18.0 g.
Result: 18.0 g.
Problem 12: Difficult Level (Full Chain: Mass → Theoretical → Percent) In the reaction 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O, 2.00 g of H2 is reacted. If 16.0 g of H2O is actually produced, what is the percent yield? (Molar masses: H2 = 2.02 g/mol, H2O = 18.02 g/mol)
Step 1 (Theoretical Yield): 2.00 g H2 ÷ 2.02 g/mol = 0.99 mol H2. 0.99 mol H2 × (2 mol H2O / 2 mol H2) = 0.99 mol H2O. 0.99 mol H2O × 18.02 g/mol = 17.84 g (Theoretical). Step 2 (Percent Yield): (16.0 g ÷ 17.84 g) × 100% = 89.7%.
Result: 89.7%.
